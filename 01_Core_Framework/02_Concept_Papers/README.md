# Reality Drift — Canonical Concept Papers

**A. Jacobs | Reality Drift Framework | 2023–2026**

This repository contains the canonical concept papers for the core ideas within the Reality Drift framework.

The collection is intended to provide stable definitions, mechanisms, relationships, and practical implications for the framework's primary concepts. The papers describe different layers of the same representational problem. Systems can remain functional, coherent, and increasingly optimized while progressively losing corrective contact with the realities they are meant to track.

At the center of the framework is a simple distinction:

> **Coherence is not contact.**

A system can continue functioning without remaining aligned.

---

## Core Framework

### Reality Drift

Reality Drift is the condition in which a system remains operational and internally coherent while the representations guiding it progressively lose corrective contact with the reality they are meant to track, describe, or govern.

Reality Drift describes the overarching condition examined throughout the framework.

**File:** `Reality_Drift_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### The Drift Principle

The **Drift Principle** states:

> **Representations can be compressed, optimized, distributed, and reused faster than real-world conditions can correct them.**

It describes the structural tendency underlying Reality Drift. Drift becomes more likely when a system's capacity to transform and circulate representations exceeds its capacity to test and revise them against their referents.

**File:** `Drift_Principle_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### Recursive Compression

Recursive Compression is the process by which systems reduce complexity into simplified representations, preserve and reuse those representations, and feed them into subsequent cycles of interpretation, modeling, or action.

Compression makes scale and cumulative intelligence possible. Recursion creates the risk that systems increasingly operate on previous representations rather than renewed contact with underlying reality.

**File:** `Recursive_Compression_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### Constraint Collapse

Constraint Collapse describes the condition in which feedback, consequence, and correction remain present but lose their ability to enforce change.

The problem is not necessarily the absence of feedback. A system can identify errors, produce reports, update metrics, and continue responding while losing the ability to invalidate its own representations or stop a misaligned process.

**File:** `Constraint_Collapse_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### The Optimization Trap

The Optimization Trap occurs when improvement within a representation becomes easier to demonstrate and reward than improvement in the underlying reality.

Metrics, dashboards, proxies, and measurable outputs can continue improving while becoming progressively less faithful to the purposes they were created to serve.

**File:** `Optimization_Trap_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

## Meaning and Representation

### Semantic Fidelity

Semantic Fidelity is the degree to which a representation preserves the meaning, intent, context, distinctions, and referential relationships necessary for its intended use.

Semantic Fidelity asks a different question from simple factual accuracy:

> Did the meaning survive the transformation?

A representation can contain accurate statements while still distorting the significance, structure, intent, or function of its source.

**File:** `Semantic_Fidelity_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### Semantic Drift

Semantic Drift is the progressive alteration or degradation of a representation's meaning or referential function as it moves across contexts, systems, summaries, incentives, or repeated reuse.

The representation may remain recognizable and coherent even as its relationship to the original meaning weakens.

**File:** `Semantic_Drift_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### Synthetic Realness

Synthetic Realness is the condition in which an optimized representation produces stronger signals of credibility, completeness, or authenticity than the less legible reality it represents.

It describes a perceptual inversion in which polish, fluency, coherence, and emotional plausibility begin functioning as substitutes for grounding.

**File:** `Synthetic_Realness_Canonical_Concept_Paper_Final_2026.pdf`

---

## Cognition and Human Experience

### Cognitive Drift

Cognitive Drift describes how human cognition can remain active, adaptive, and internally coherent while progressively losing alignment with reality as it becomes increasingly dependent on compressed representations, mediated inputs, and recursive internal models.

The concept extends Reality Drift from systems into human cognition.

**File:** `Cognitive_Drift_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### Co-Cognition

Co-Cognition is a structural process in which human cognition and external systems, particularly AI systems, operate within a shared feedback loop, jointly generating, refining, and applying representations.

Human thought becomes partially distributed across the interaction.

A bounded co-cognitive loop can increase clarity while preserving human intent, testing, judgment, and stopping conditions. An unbounded loop can amplify Cognitive Drift by returning increasingly coherent representations without corresponding increases in grounding.

**File:** `Co_Cognition_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### Filter Fatigue

Filter Fatigue is the cognitive exhaustion produced by continuous relevance filtering in representation-heavy environments.

As personalization, weak provenance, abundant information, and plausible synthetic outputs increase, individuals must repeatedly determine what is real, relevant, original, trustworthy, or sufficiently grounded.

The burden of maintaining context increasingly shifts from the system to the individual.

**File:** `Filter_Fatigue_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

### The Drifted Self

The Drifted Self describes the condition in which identity remains functional and adaptive while progressively losing continuity, grounding, and internal coherence under accelerated, mediated environments.

The paper examines four related dimensions:

- **Identity Drift**
- **Authenticity Drift**
- **Performativity Drift**
- **Temporal Drift**

The Drifted Self describes what Reality Drift can feel like when systemic conditions become internalized within identity and everyday experience.

**File:** `Drifted_Self_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

## Time and System Synchronization

### Temporal Drift

Temporal Drift is the loss of alignment between the timing, pace, sequence, or memory of a representational system and the real-world processes it is meant to track.

Systems can remain active and responsive while becoming temporally misaligned because:

- representations update faster than underlying reality
- consequences arrive after decisions have propagated
- different layers operate on incompatible time horizons
- real processes are compressed into periodic metrics or snapshots

Temporal Drift extends Reality Drift into the dimension of time.

**File:** `Temporal_Drift_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

---

## Macro-Level Condition

### The Age of Drift

The Age of Drift is the systemic condition in which Reality Drift becomes the default operating environment across institutions, technologies, culture, and human cognition.

It is the macro-level frame for the interaction of the framework's major dynamics.

Systems continue functioning, scaling, optimizing, and generating coherent outputs, but representation increasingly outruns correction.

The result is not necessarily collapse.

It is continued operation under weakening alignment.

**File:** `Age_of_Drift_Canonical_Concept_Paper_v1_Final_2026.pdf`

## How the Concepts Fit Together

The papers describe different levels of the same system:

**The Drift Principle**  
↓  
Representations can move faster than reality can correct them.

**Recursive Compression**  
↓  
Representations become inputs to later representations.

**Semantic Drift**  
↓  
Meaning changes across those transformations.

**Semantic Fidelity**  
↓  
Measures how much meaning survives.

**The Optimization Trap**  
↓  
Internal representations become easier to improve than underlying reality.

**Constraint Collapse**  
↓  
Feedback remains present but loses corrective force.

**Reality Drift**  
↓  
Systems remain functional while losing corrective contact with reality.

From there, the same dynamics appear at other levels:

- **Synthetic Realness** describes the perceptual layer.
- **Temporal Drift** describes the time layer.
- **Cognitive Drift** describes the cognitive layer.
- **Co-Cognition** describes human-system cognitive loops.
- **Filter Fatigue** describes the filtering burden imposed on individuals.
- **The Drifted Self** describes the identity-level consequences.
- **The Age of Drift** describes the macro-level environment in which these processes increasingly occur together.

## Core Propositions

Across the collection, several propositions recur:

- Systems do not need to fail in order to become misaligned.
- Internal coherence does not guarantee external grounding.
- Representations can improve while their relationship to reality deteriorates.
- Feedback can remain visible while losing its ability to enforce correction.
- Compression is necessary for complex systems, but recursive compression can compound loss.
- Meaning can degrade without obvious factual error.
- Optimization can preserve apparent performance while weakening the relationship between metrics and purpose.
- Human cognition increasingly operates inside the same representational systems it is attempting to evaluate.
- Restoring alignment requires renewed contact with underlying conditions, not simply additional representation.

---

## Central Formulations

> The absence of failure is not proof of alignment.

> Coherence is not contact.

> Representations can be compressed, optimized, distributed, and reused faster than real-world conditions can correct them.

> Most systems don't break. They drift.
