# Reality Drift: Core Mechanisms and Systemic Patterns

This collection examines how modern systems gradually lose contact with reality while remaining functional, coherent, legible, and internally defensible.

The papers focus on the mechanisms that transform lived conditions into representations, categories, metrics, procedures, narratives, policies, and models. Across institutions, organizations, AI systems, bureaucracies, and cultural environments, these representations can become increasingly self-reinforcing while becoming less answerable to the reality they were created to describe.

The recurring pattern is:

**reality → representation → simplification → optimization → recursive reuse → weakened correction → drift**

The collection develops the broader framework of Reality Drift through concepts including Semantic Fidelity, Representational Hardening, Recursive Compression, Temporal Drift, Narrative Drift, Bureaucratic Drift, Institutional Drift, Mission Drift, Process Drift, and Policy Drift.

## Central Principle

A system can continue working after it has stopped learning from reality.

Its procedures may complete.  
Its metrics may improve.  
Its representations may become clearer.  
Its narratives may become more coherent.

None of these are proof that the system remains aligned with what is actually happening.

Coherence is not contact.

The absence of failure is not proof of alignment.
