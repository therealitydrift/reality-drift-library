# Reality Drift Taxonomy

**A. Jacobs | Reality Drift Framework | 2023–2026**

> The absence of failure is not proof of alignment.  
> Coherence is not contact.

## Overview

This collection defines five related forms of drift within the Reality Drift framework:

1. Institutional Drift

2. Mission Drift

3. Bureaucratic Drift

4. Narrative Drift

5. Temporal Drift

Reality Drift describes the progressive loss of corrective contact between a system’s representations and the real-world conditions they are meant to track, explain, or affect, even while the system remains functional and internally coherent.

These papers separate that broader pattern into distinct but interacting forms.

The purpose of the taxonomy is not to describe ordinary error, disagreement, inefficiency, bias, or institutional failure.

It is to identify situations in which a system can continue operating successfully according to its own representations while becoming progressively less answerable to the reality those representations were created to organize.

## The General Structure

Across the taxonomy, the same basic relationship appears:

```text
REAL-WORLD CONDITION
        ↓
REPRESENTATION
        ↓
DECISION / ACTION
        ↓
SYSTEM RESPONSE
        ↓
NEW REPRESENTATION
        ↓
CORRECTION OR DRIFT
```

Representations are necessary.

Institutions cannot operate without categories, procedures, narratives, records, metrics, timelines, mission statements, and models.

Drift begins when those representations become less correctable by the underlying conditions they are supposed to represent.

The system may remain:

- active

- coherent

- measurable

- procedurally valid

- narratively stable

- institutionally legitimate

while corrective contact weakens.

# 1. Institutional Drift

**When Institutional Representations Remain Authoritative After Losing Corrective Contact**

Institutional Drift is the broad organizational expression of Reality Drift.

It describes the progressive movement of an institution away from its stated purpose or real-world function while its procedures, outputs, and internal legitimacy continue.

The institution still operates.

Its reports are produced.  
Its policies remain active.  
Its metrics continue updating.  
Its categories remain authoritative.  
Its procedures remain valid.

But the representations through which the institution understands and governs reality become progressively less answerable to the conditions the institution exists to address.

## Core mechanisms

- representational compression

- proxy optimization

- Recursive Compression

- feedback weakening

- Constraint Collapse

## Recognition pattern

```text
EXTERNAL CONDITION
        ↓
INSTITUTIONAL REPRESENTATION
        ↓
POLICY / PROCEDURE / ACTION
        ↓
ALTERED ENVIRONMENT
        ↓
MEASURED THROUGH SAME REPRESENTATION
        ↓
APPARENT CONFIRMATION
```

The institution increasingly encounters a world already shaped by its own categories and actions.

Internal coherence can therefore increase while the evidentiary path back to the original condition becomes harder to reconstruct.

Institutional Drift can contain several more specific pathways:

```text
INSTITUTIONAL DRIFT
│
├── Mission Drift
├── Bureaucratic Drift
├── Narrative Drift
└── Temporal Drift
```

# 2. Mission Drift

**When Stated Purpose Remains Visible but Loses Its Power to Constrain Decisions**

Mission Drift is the purpose-level expression of Reality Drift.

It occurs when the stated mission of an organization remains visible and continues supplying identity and legitimacy, but other objectives increasingly determine actual decisions.

These substitute objectives may include:

- growth

- revenue

- funding

- rankings

- enrollment

- efficiency

- visibility

- organizational survival

The mission does not necessarily disappear.

It may become more visible.

Mission language can remain prominent in branding, strategic plans, speeches, grant applications, and public communication even as the mission loses its power to constrain behavior.

## Core mechanisms

- purpose compression

- proxy substitution

- narrative preservation

- gradual normalization

- Constraint Collapse

The critical distinction is between **mission change **and Mission Drift.

A mission can be openly revised in response to changing conditions.

Mission Drift occurs when the legitimating purpose remains formally intact while different objectives become operationally decisive.

```text
STATED PURPOSE
      ↓
MISSION REPRESENTATION
      ↓
SECONDARY OBJECTIVE
      ↓
DECISION PRESSURE
      ↓
SECONDARY OBJECTIVE BECOMES PRIMARY
      ↓
MISSION REMAINS AS LEGITIMATION
```

The mission remains visible as a representation while losing its force as a constraint.

# 3. Bureaucratic Drift

**When Proof of Process Substitutes for Proof of Resolution**

Bureaucratic Drift is the procedural expression of Reality Drift.

It is the progressive substitution of authorized process for substantive resolution.

Procedures, approvals, records, compliance checks, workflows, and completion states may all function correctly while the condition that justified the process remains unresolved.

The central substitution is:

```text
PROOF OF PROCESS
        ↓
BECOMES
        ↓
PROOF OF RESOLUTION
```

A form is completed.

A case is closed.

An approval is recorded.

A required meeting occurs.

A compliance check passes.

Yet none of these necessarily demonstrate that the underlying condition changed.

## Core mechanisms

- procedural substitution

- verification substitution

- procedural completion without resolution

- responsibility fragmentation

- rule accumulation

- Constraint Collapse

Bureaucratic Drift becomes especially durable when completion ends the institution's obligation to look again.

Once the required fields, approvals, records, and actions exist, the representation of successful action can become institutionally stronger than the unresolved condition outside the workflow.

Every participant may have completed a valid local task while nobody remains responsible for the whole outcome.

# 4. Narrative Drift

**When Explanations Remain Coherent After Losing the Ability to Be Corrected by Events**

Narrative Drift is the interpretive expression of Reality Drift.

It describes the progressive loss of corrective contact between an explanatory account and the events, evidence, or causal conditions that account claims to describe.

The narrative can remain:

- coherent

- persuasive

- recognizable

- institutionally useful

- internally consistent

while becoming increasingly resistant to revision.

Contradictory evidence does not necessarily destroy the narrative.

Instead, contradiction may be:

- reframed

- absorbed

- reclassified

- excluded

- detached from provenance

- incorporated as further support

## Core mechanisms

- coherence preservation

- retrospective fitting

- Recursive Compression

- synthetic coherence

- feedback insulation

- narrative absorption

A particularly important pattern appears when narratives begin affecting the conditions they later claim merely to describe.

```text
NARRATIVE
    ↓
CLASSIFICATION
    ↓
ATTENTION
    ↓
MEASUREMENT
    ↓
INSTITUTIONAL ACTION
    ↓
ALTERED ENVIRONMENT
    ↓
INTERPRETED THROUGH ORIGINAL NARRATIVE
```

The narrative begins helping produce the evidence later used to confirm it.

Coherence can therefore increase even as corrective contact weakens.

Narrative Drift is not synonymous with falsehood, bias, propaganda, or ordinary disagreement.

An inaccurate explanation can still remain open to correction.

Drift begins when the narrative increasingly determines how contradiction itself will be interpreted.

---

# 5. Temporal Drift

**When Representational Time Separates from the Timing of Underlying Reality**

Temporal Drift is the time-based expression of Reality Drift.

It occurs when the timing, pace, sequence, or memory of a representational system becomes misaligned with the real-world process it is meant to track.

The problem may involve:

- representations updating too quickly

- representations updating too slowly

- old records retaining authority

- events losing their original sequence

- consequences arriving after decision cycles close

- newer summaries overwriting earlier uncertainty

- present states replacing the history needed to interpret them

## Core mechanisms

- pace mismatch

- sequence loss

- persistence mismatch

- memory overwrite

- Recursive Compression

- feedback delay

A system can appear extremely responsive because its representations refresh constantly while remaining poorly aligned with the actual timescale of the condition being measured.

```text
REAL PROCESS
───────────────►

REPRESENTATION
─►─►─►─►─►─►─►

FAST UPDATES ≠ TEMPORAL ALIGNMENT
```

Update speed is not the same as contact.

A rapidly refreshed dashboard may still represent the wrong temporal layer.

A current record may preserve assumptions from conditions that no longer exist.

A feed may present events without preserving the sequence needed to interpret causality.

An institutional summary may preserve the latest conclusion while erasing the uncertainty, reversals, and failed predictions that produced it.

Temporal Drift therefore weakens Semantic Fidelity by removing sequence, duration, timing, and historical context.

# How the Forms Interact

These forms of drift are analytically distinct but rarely isolated.

They can reinforce one another.

```text
MISSION DRIFT
Purpose stops constraining decisions
        ↓
BUREAUCRATIC DRIFT
Process substitutes for resolution
        ↓
NARRATIVE DRIFT
Explanation preserves legitimacy
        ↓
TEMPORAL DRIFT
Sequence and history disappear
        ↓
INSTITUTIONAL DRIFT
The organization remains coherent
while corrective contact weakens
```

The direction does not have to follow this exact order.

Any pathway can stabilize another.

For example:

- Mission Drift can be hidden by Narrative Drift.

- Bureaucratic Drift can convert substantive failure into valid completion.

- Narrative Drift can preserve Institutional Drift by explaining away contradictory outcomes.

- Temporal Drift can erase the sequence required to recognize that a narrative changed.

- Institutional Drift can preserve all four by maintaining the authority of the representations through which they operate.

## Taxonomy at a Glance

| Drift Form          | What Loses Contact                              | What Remains Intact                    |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Institutional Drift | Institution ↔ external purpose or condition     | Institutional operation and legitimacy |
| Mission Drift       | Decisions ↔ stated purpose                      | Mission language and identity          |
| Bureaucratic Drift  | Process ↔ substantive resolution                | Procedural completion                  |
| Narrative Drift     | Explanation ↔ events and evidence               | Narrative coherence                    |
| Temporal Drift      | Representation ↔ real-world timing and sequence | Current representation                 |

## The Common Failure

Each form involves a different representation becoming increasingly capable of surviving contradiction.

```text
MISSION
can survive purpose loss

PROCEDURE
can survive unresolved outcomes

NARRATIVE
can survive contradictory evidence

RECORD
can survive changed conditions

INSTITUTION
can survive all of the above
```

This is why visible continuation is weak evidence of alignment.

The important question is not simply whether a system still works according to its own rules.

The important question is whether reality can still force those rules, categories, narratives, procedures, and objectives to change.

## Compact Reality Drift Diagnostic

Across the taxonomy, five questions recur:

1. **What is the real-world referent?**  
   What condition, purpose, event, outcome, or process is the system supposed to remain answerable to?

2. **What representation stands in for it?**  
   What metric, procedure, narrative, record, category, timeline, or mission statement organizes decisions?

3. **What behavior does the representation reward?**  
   What becomes easier, safer, more legible, more profitable, or more institutionally successful?

4. **What can still force revision?**  
   What evidence has enough authority to change the representation itself?

5. **Can the system succeed while the referent deteriorates?**  
   If so, internal success may no longer provide strong evidence of external alignment.

## Boundary

Reality Drift does not mean that abstraction, bureaucracy, institutions, missions, narratives, or records are inherently defective.

These representations make complex coordination possible.

Nor does drift require deliberate deception, corruption, incompetence, or bad intent.

Competent participants can produce drift while faithfully following rules, incentives, categories, and procedures that no longer preserve corrective contact.

The defining problem is not imperfection.

It is the weakening of the relationship through which reality can still revise the representation.
