# Organizational and Institutional Drift

This directory contains notes on how organizations, institutions, narratives, and shared timelines can remain functional while gradually losing alignment with the realities they were created to respond to.

The common pattern is not sudden collapse.

The system continues operating. Procedures remain in place. Narratives remain coherent. Missions remain visible. Activity continues.

What weakens is the relationship between those structures and the external conditions they were meant to track.

## Included Notes

### Bureaucratic Drift: When Processes Expand and Outcomes Stall

Examines how procedures, approvals, compliance systems, and administrative layers can expand until process begins substituting for outcome.

The organization remains structured and operational, but more effort is absorbed by managing the system itself while responsiveness and real-world effectiveness weaken.

### Mission Drift: When Organizations Lose Their Original Purpose

Explores how organizations gradually move away from their founding purpose as growth, revenue, efficiency, funding, or survival pressures become more influential.

The mission may remain visible in branding and communication while losing its role as a real constraint on decisions.

### Narrative Drift: When Explanations Detach From Reality

Examines how explanations and stories can remain coherent even as the conditions they describe change.

Narratives may be preserved through reinterpretation, abstraction, selective feedback, and retrospective fitting until explanation becomes more self-referential than reality-responsive.

### Institutional Drift: When Organizations Lose Alignment With Reality

Provides the broader organizational pattern connecting metrics, incentives, feedback distortion, recursive abstraction, and proxy optimization.

Institutions can continue producing consistent outputs and positive internal signals while becoming less responsive to the environments they were created to serve.

### Temporal Drift: The Collapse of Shared Time in Modern Systems

Examines what happens when systems remain active but lose synchronization with shared sequence, context, and continuity.

Information arrives asynchronously, events are processed out of order, and continuous updates overwrite context before it can stabilize into shared understanding.

## Core Pattern

Across these notes, a similar sequence appears:

**purpose or reality → representation → internal structure → insulation → drift**

An organization begins with a mission.

A bureaucracy creates procedures.

An institution creates metrics and reporting systems.

A narrative organizes events into an explanation.

A shared timeline organizes events into sequence.

These structures make coordination possible.

Over time, however, they can become increasingly self-sustaining.

The mission becomes language.

The procedure becomes the goal.

The metric becomes evidence of success.

The narrative preserves coherence.

The timeline fragments into updates.

The system remains recognizable while its grounding weakens.

## Different Forms of the Same Problem

These drift types describe different layers of the same larger failure.

**Mission drift** concerns purpose.

**Bureaucratic drift** concerns process.

**Institutional drift** concerns organizational alignment.

**Narrative drift** concerns explanation.

**Temporal drift** concerns sequence and shared context.

Each can reinforce the others.

A drifting institution may preserve an outdated mission statement.

Expanded bureaucracy may make correction slower.

Narrative drift may explain away poor outcomes.

Temporal fragmentation may make contradictions harder to compare across time.

Together, these mechanisms can allow a system to remain coherent internally while becoming increasingly detached externally.

## Relationship to Reality Drift

Reality Drift describes the broader condition in which systems remain operational and internally coherent while losing corrective contact with real-world conditions.

These notes examine how that process appears at the organizational and institutional level.

The key warning is that visible continuity does not guarantee alignment.

A system can still have rules, meetings, reports, goals, explanations, and constant activity while becoming progressively less answerable to the reality those structures were meant to serve.
