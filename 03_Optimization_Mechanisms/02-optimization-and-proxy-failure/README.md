# Reality Drift — Metrics, Proxies, Incentives, and Representation

A. Jacobs | Reality Drift Framework | 2023–2026

This collection examines one of the central pathways into Reality Drift: the transformation of reality into metrics, proxies, specifications, procedures, and operational representations that gradually acquire authority over the conditions they were created to describe.

The papers connect Goodhart’s Law, Campbell’s Law, principal-agent problems, KPI distortion, proxy optimization, and the map-territory distinction to the broader Reality Drift framework.

The central problem is not simply that measurements become inaccurate.

It is that representations can remain operational after their relationship to reality has weakened.

> The absence of failure is not proof of alignment.

> Coherence is not contact.

---

## Goodhart’s Law, Campbell’s Law, and Reality Drift

This paper examines how metric corruption can expand into a larger failure of representational correction.

Goodhart’s Law describes what happens when measures become targets. Campbell’s Law describes the distortion pressure placed on quantitative indicators when they become consequential.

Reality Drift extends the analysis beyond the initial corruption of the measure.

A degraded representation can continue to allocate resources, organize decisions, structure behavior, and circulate through larger systems even after its validity has weakened.

The paper develops the sequence:

Reality → Representation → Operational Proxy → Optimization → Constraint Weakening → Feedback Substitution → Fidelity Loss → Reality Drift

It also introduces Survivable Wrongness, where a misleading representation remains usable enough for the system to continue operating, preventing misalignment from automatically producing correction.

A further stage occurs when the representation helps reshape the environment and that altered environment is later interpreted as independent confirmation of the representation itself:

Reality → Measure → Target → Behavioral Adaptation → Altered Reality → Recapture → Apparent Confirmation

## Performance Metrics, KPI Distortion, and Campbell’s Law

This paper is a diagnostic guide to indicators that remain successful after they stop preserving the outcomes they were intended to represent.

It begins with the referent, the real condition or outcome a metric is supposed to preserve.

A metric becomes dangerous when improving the indicator becomes easier than improving the underlying condition, while the indicator continues governing action.

The paper traces KPI distortion through eight stages:

1. Observation
2. Standardization
3. Consequence
4. Behavioral adaptation
5. Proxy substitution
6. Feedback weakening
7. Constraint Collapse
8. Survivable Wrongness

It also places metrics inside the wider Representation Stack:

Reality → Measurement → Metric → Operational Representation → Optimization → Narrative

Each layer can remain technically correct while the chain as a whole becomes less faithful to the underlying outcome.

The paper includes diagnostic questions for determining whether a measurement system still preserves corrective contact with reality and emphasizes that adding more indicators does not necessarily solve the problem.

The objective is not to eliminate measurement.

It is to prevent the measurement system from becoming self-validating.

## Principal-Agent Problems, Incentive Misalignment, and Reality Drift

This paper examines what happens when delegated systems become increasingly answerable to their own representations of success.

Delegation creates an unavoidable representational problem.

Principals cannot directly observe everything performed on their behalf, so they rely on reports, metrics, procedures, audits, and compliance records.

The problem becomes Reality Drift when those representations increasingly determine how the principal understands the delegated system while evidence from the underlying purpose loses the power to correct it.

The paper introduces the Delegation Stack:

Purpose → Delegated Mandate → Specification → Procedure → Metric or Report → Evidence of Alignment

As the chain develops, the original purpose becomes progressively compressed into representations produced inside the delegated system.

The paper distinguishes formal accountability from binding accountability.

Formal accountability produces evidence that oversight occurred.

Binding accountability creates consequences capable of changing behavior or revising the governing representation.

At the deepest stage, representational inversion occurs when the principal must understand the underlying reality primarily through the agent’s account of it.

The agent defines the categories, records the activity, interprets the outputs, and proposes the corrective action.

Oversight remains formally external while becoming informationally dependent on the system it was meant to constrain.

## Proxy Optimization and Reality Drift

This paper provides a reference guide to proxy optimization within the Reality Drift framework.

A proxy is a measurable or operational stand-in for a condition that cannot be handled directly.

Proxy Optimization occurs when behavior becomes organized around improving the stand-in rather than reliably improving the condition it represents.

The paper distinguishes several stages and related concepts:

- Proxy substitution — the stand-in becomes the practical object of the system
- Proxy Optimization — behavior becomes organized around improving the stand-in
- The Optimization Trap — further proxy improvement progressively worsens alignment
- Representational inversion — reality is reorganized to satisfy the representation
- Reality Drift — the resulting misalignment persists while system operation continues

The guide also distinguishes Proxy Optimization from several adjacent phenomena:

- Goodhart effects
- Metric gaming
- Reward hacking
- Specification gaming
- Goal displacement
- Signal distortion

These are not treated as identical failures. They are different ways in which optimization can move toward the representation rather than the underlying purpose.

The paper connects proxy optimization to the Representation Stack and the Drift Principle, showing why proxies can become embedded in budgets, workflows, expectations, and automated decisions before slower real-world consequences become visible.

## What Does “The Map Is Not the Territory” Actually Mean?

This paper examines the foundational distinction between reality and representation.

Every representation selects, compresses, and organizes reality rather than reproducing it completely.

The danger begins when the representation becomes operational.

Metrics allocate resources. Categories determine eligibility. Models guide intervention. Forecasts alter behavior.

The map begins participating in the production of the territory it supposedly only describes.

The paper emphasizes that accuracy is always relative to purpose.

A representation can accurately capture a selected feature while failing to preserve the larger condition it stands for.

> Accuracy at one layer does not guarantee fidelity across the representational chain.

The paper connects the map-territory distinction to:

- Semantic Fidelity
- Reification
- Operational representations
- The Representation Stack
- Recursive Compression
- Goodhart effects
- Representational inversion
- Reality Drift

A particularly important transition occurs when people adapt their behavior to the representation.

At that point, the map becomes one of the forces shaping the territory it will later measure.

The full recursive sequence is:

Reality → Capture → Representation → Operational Proxy → Optimization → Behavioral Adaptation → Altered Reality → Recapture → Apparent Confirmation

The danger is no longer simply that the map is incomplete.

The map can begin controlling access to the territory, reshaping it, and then treating the altered territory as confirmation of itself.

# How the Five Papers Fit Together

These papers examine the same structural problem from different entry points.

## 1. The Map-Territory Problem

Reality cannot be used directly at scale.

Systems therefore create representations.

Reality → Representation

The representation necessarily preserves some features while excluding others.

## 2. Measurement

Representations become operational metrics, classifications, models, procedures, and reports.

Reality → Measurement → Metric → Operational Representation

At this stage, the representation is still intended to remain answerable to the condition beneath it.

## 3. Consequence

Once resources, status, access, evaluation, or reward depend on the representation, behavior begins adapting to it.

This is where Goodhart’s Law and Campbell’s Law become central.

The measure no longer merely observes the environment.

It becomes part of the environment.

## 4. Proxy Optimization

The representation becomes the practical object of optimization.

Underlying condition → Proxy → Optimization

The system can become increasingly effective at improving the proxy without producing equivalent improvement in the referent.

## 5. Incentive Adaptation

Actors learn what the oversight system recognizes.

In principal-agent systems, the agent may become more responsive to the representation of success than to the original purpose.

Purpose → Mandate → Specification → Procedure → Report → Evidence of Alignment

## 6. Feedback Weakening

Contradictory evidence still exists, but its influence declines.

It may arrive too late, remain outside the dashboard, be translated into the same categories already producing the problem, or be explained without changing the system.

## 7. Constraint Collapse

Correction is still formally possible, but contradiction can no longer force revision.

The system can acknowledge evidence while continuing unchanged.

## 8. Survivable Wrongness

Misalignment persists because the system can absorb it.

The representation still performs enough operational work to preserve continuity.

Nothing necessarily breaks.

## 9. Representational Inversion

The representation begins shaping the reality it originally described.

Reality → Proxy → Optimization → Behavioral Adaptation → Altered Reality

The altered environment is then measured again.

## 10. Apparent Confirmation

The system encounters the effects of its own intervention and interprets them as independent evidence.

Altered Reality → Recapture → Apparent Confirmation

The representation has become part of a self-reinforcing loop.

## 11. Reality Drift

At this stage, the central problem is no longer simply metric distortion, incentive misalignment, or an imperfect proxy.

The larger system remains functional while the representations organizing it lose sufficient corrective contact with the conditions they were created to serve.

That is Reality Drift.

# The Representation Stack

A recurring structure across the collection is:

Reality  
↓  
Measurement  
↓  
Metric  
↓  
Operational Representation  
↓  
Optimization  
↓  
Narrative

Each transformation solves a coordination problem while introducing characteristic loss.

- Measurement gains visibility while excluding context.
- Metrics gain comparability while reducing granularity.
- Operational representations gain usability while obscuring assumptions and provenance.
- Optimization gains direction while excluding unmodeled tradeoffs.
- Narrative gains coordination while potentially closing uncertainty around transformed outputs.

The danger appears when fidelity across the stack is assumed simply because every individual layer remains operational.

# Core Propositions

- A representation is never identical to the reality it represents.
- Metrics can remain accurate at the level of measurement while losing fidelity to the broader outcome.
- Once representations become consequential, behavior adapts to them.
- Optimization changes what signals mean.
- A proxy can improve while the underlying condition deteriorates.
- Formal compliance does not establish substantive alignment.
- Oversight can remain visible while corrective authority disappears.
- Representations can shape the environments later used to validate them.
- Internal success signals cannot independently prove alignment.
- A functioning system can remain deeply misaligned.
- Continued operation is not evidence that correction still works.

---

# Central Formulations

> The absence of failure is not proof of alignment.

> Coherence is not contact.

> Accuracy at one layer does not guarantee fidelity across the representational chain.

> Goodhart’s Law describes what happens when measures become targets. Reality Drift describes what happens when representations of any kind begin displacing the realities they were created to represent.

> Representations can be compressed, optimized, distributed, and reused faster than reality can correct them.
