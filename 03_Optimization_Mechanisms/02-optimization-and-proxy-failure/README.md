# Representational Failure and Proxy Optimization

This directory contains notes and reference guides on how representations such as metrics, KPIs, categories, reports, incentives, and delegated systems can become detached from the realities they were created to represent.

The common pattern is simple: a system cannot act directly on a complex reality, so it creates a simplified representation. That representation may begin as a useful guide. Once incentives, decisions, accountability, or optimization pressure become attached to it, however, the system can begin adapting to the representation itself.

At that point, the proxy may remain visible, measurable, and operational even as its relationship to the underlying reality weakens.

## Included Notes

### Goodhart’s Law, Campbell’s Law, and Reality Drift

Examines how measures become less reliable once systems begin organizing around them. Goodhart’s Law describes how measures degrade when they become targets, while Campbell’s Law focuses on the distortion created when quantitative indicators are used for high-stakes decision-making.

The broader Reality Drift problem begins when the corrupted measure continues organizing the system after its connection to reality has weakened.

### Performance Metrics, KPI Distortion, and Campbell’s Law

Focuses on indicator drift inside organizations. Metrics, dashboards, rankings, and KPIs make complex conditions easier to observe and manage, but they can also become substitutes for those conditions.

As incentives attach to the indicator, behavior reorganizes around what the measurement rewards.

### Principal-Agent Problems, Incentive Misalignment, and Reality Drift

Explores how delegated systems can drift away from the goals they were created to pursue.

Delegation requires principals to rely on representations such as reports, metrics, procedures, and accountability systems. As information asymmetry and incentive adaptation increase, agents can become more responsive to those representations than to the original purpose.

### Proxy Optimization and Reality Drift

Provides a cross-domain guide to proxy optimization, Goodhart’s Law, metric gaming, reward hacking, specification gaming, goal displacement, and signal distortion.

The central failure occurs when a system becomes better at improving the representation of success than achieving the underlying objective.

### What Does “The Map Is Not the Territory” Actually Mean?

Provides the foundational representational problem behind the rest of the set.

Every representation selects, simplifies, and organizes reality. Problems become more serious when representations become operational and begin shaping decisions, incentives, behavior, and the evidence later used to evaluate the system.

## Core Pattern

Across these notes, the same sequence appears:

**reality → representation → consequence → adaptation → substitution → drift**

A metric represents an outcome.

A KPI represents organizational performance.

A report represents delegated activity.

A category represents a more complicated condition.

A reward function represents an objective.

Once consequences attach to the representation, behavior begins adapting around it.

Eventually, the system may treat movement in the representation as evidence that reality itself has improved.

## From Map to System

The deeper problem is not simply that representations are incomplete.

All representations are incomplete.

The problem begins when the representation becomes part of the environment it was created to describe.

People adapt to metrics. Organizations adapt to reporting systems. Agents adapt to incentives. Platforms adapt to engagement signals. AI systems adapt to reward functions.

The map begins changing the territory.

Once that happens, the representation can become partially self-confirming. Behavior shaped by the representation produces new evidence that appears to validate the representation itself.

## Relationship to Reality Drift

Reality Drift describes what happens when representations continue organizing action while becoming progressively less answerable to the realities they were created to represent.

Goodhart effects, Campbell effects, KPI distortion, principal-agent problems, proxy optimization, and map-territory failures are different entry points into this broader problem.

The common failure is not representation itself.

The failure occurs when the representation remains authoritative after its corrective relationship with reality has weakened.
