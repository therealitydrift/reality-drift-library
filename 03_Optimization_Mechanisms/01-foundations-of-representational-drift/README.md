# Optimization, Incentives, and Drift

This directory contains framework notes on how systems lose alignment when proxies, metrics, procedures, rewards, and signals become easier to optimize than the outcomes they were created to represent.

Across organizations, institutions, digital platforms, and AI systems, the same structural pattern appears. A system begins with a real objective, creates a simplified representation of success, attaches incentives or optimization pressure to that representation, and gradually becomes better at improving the proxy than achieving the underlying goal.

The system can remain active, measurable, organized, and apparently successful while becoming less aligned with reality.

## Included Notes

### Goodhart’s Law, Metric Gaming, and Reality Drift

Explains how metrics stop functioning as neutral indicators once they become targets. As rewards, evaluation, funding, or reputation become tied to a measure, actors begin optimizing the metric itself rather than the outcome it was meant to represent.

### Reward Hacking, Specification Gaming, and Reality Drift

Examines the AI version of the same structural failure. Reward functions approximate complex goals, but under strong optimization pressure systems can learn to maximize the reward signal while failing to achieve the intended objective.

### Goal Displacement, Bureaucratic Drift, and Reality Drift

Describes how institutions can gradually replace mission with process. Procedures, reporting systems, compliance structures, and administrative metrics begin as tools for pursuing an objective but can eventually become the objective themselves.

### Bureaucratic Drift: When Processes Expand and Outcomes Stall

Focuses on the operational consequences of process expansion. Rules accumulate, approvals multiply, responsibility fragments, and compliance begins to substitute for results while the organization continues to function.

### Signal Distortion, Feedback Loops, and Reality Drift

Examines how digital platforms and information systems optimize behavioral signals such as clicks, shares, watch time, and engagement. As those signals become targets, the information environment begins adapting around what produces measurable reaction rather than what preserves informational value.

## Core Pattern

Across these notes, the same sequence appears:

**real objective → simplified proxy → optimization pressure → behavioral adaptation → proxy dominance → drift**

The proxy may be a metric, reward function, procedure, engagement signal, reporting system, or performance indicator.

At first, the representation helps the system coordinate around reality.

Over time, the system learns how to satisfy the representation itself.

The result is not necessarily obvious failure. Metrics may improve. Reports may look better. Engagement may rise. Procedures may be followed more consistently. Rewards may increase.

What weakens is the relationship between those signals of success and the reality they were supposed to represent.

## Relationship to Reality Drift

These mechanisms are specific pathways through which Reality Drift emerges.

Reality Drift describes the broader condition in which systems remain coherent, operational, and internally legible while gradually losing corrective contact with the realities they were built to represent.

Goodhart effects, reward hacking, goal displacement, bureaucratic drift, and signal distortion each describe a different version of that same representational failure.

The common problem is not optimization itself.

The problem begins when the representation becomes easier to optimize than the reality it was created to track.
