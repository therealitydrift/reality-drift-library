# What Is Reality Drift?

Reality Drift is a framework for understanding how systems can remain operational, internally coherent, and apparently successful while becoming progressively less answerable to the realities they were created to represent, govern, or serve. It describes an accumulating loss of corrective contact rather than a single mistake or sudden breakdown. Metrics, models, categories, procedures, narratives, interfaces, and other representations can continue organizing action even as their relationship to underlying conditions weakens.

The central problem is not representation itself. Complex systems cannot act on reality in its full continuity and context, so they depend on representations that make conditions measurable, comparable, communicable, and actionable. A patient becomes a record, learning becomes a score, judgment becomes a metric, a social relationship becomes engagement data, and an institutional purpose becomes a performance indicator. These transformations are necessary, but they are also selective. Every representation preserves some features while leaving others out, and every additional layer of abstraction creates more distance between the system and the conditions it is trying to understand or manage.

Reality Drift begins when representational change starts to outpace reality-based correction. A system may continue producing valid outputs according to its own rules while becoming less responsive to the reality beyond those rules. This is why the absence of visible failure is not proof of alignment. A system can be productive, measurable, procedurally compliant, internally consistent, and technically functional while becoming increasingly misaligned with what it exists to track, explain, improve, or govern. Coherence is not contact.

## How Drift Develops

The framework centers on a recurring sequence in which reality is translated into representations, those representations become operational proxies, and the proxies become targets for optimization. As attention and resources shift toward what the system can measure and improve, conditions outside the representation can lose influence. Direct observation, local knowledge, professional judgment, lived experience, contradictory evidence, downstream consequences, and material limits may still exist, but they matter less if they no longer have enough authority to alter what the system does.

This can lead to feedback substitution, where internal forms of verification begin replacing external correction. Dashboards validate dashboards, procedures validate compliance, benchmarks validate models, and successful completion becomes evidence of successful resolution. The system increasingly evaluates itself through outputs generated inside the same representational environment. Over time, the representation can preserve its form while losing fidelity to its original purpose, meaning, or referent. At that point, misalignment becomes compatible with continued operation rather than producing immediate failure.

A compact version of this process is:

**Reality → Representation → Proxy → Optimization → Constraint Weakening → Feedback Substitution → Fidelity Loss → Reality Drift**

The important point is that optimization alone does not produce Reality Drift. The problem emerges when optimization becomes stronger than the feedback and constraints required to keep the representation answerable to reality.

## The Drift Principle

The Drift Principle describes the broader structural tendency underneath the framework. Representation, compression, and optimization can scale faster than the feedback and constraints needed to keep them aligned with their referents. What scales is usually not reality itself, but a representation of it. Measurements move more easily than lived conditions, metrics move more easily than purposes, categories move more easily than ambiguity, models move more easily than the situations from which they were derived, and summaries move more easily than sources.

As these representations become easier to store, compare, transmit, optimize, and reuse, systems increasingly operate on prior representations rather than returning to the underlying conditions. This does not make representation inherently unreliable. It means that alignment depends on maintaining a route back to what the representation stands for and preserving the ability of external conditions to force revision.

## The Representation Stack

The Representation Stack is a reference model for tracing how reality becomes operational inside a system. A simplified sequence is:

**Reality → Measurement → Metrics → Operational Representation → Optimization → Narrative**

Each layer solves a coordination problem while introducing its own form of loss. Measurement creates visibility but excludes what is not captured. Metrics create comparability but reduce granularity and qualitative difference. Operational representations make complexity usable but can hide process, provenance, uncertainty, and assumptions. Optimization creates direction but can separate performance from original purpose. Narrative creates shared meaning but can close uncertainty and conceal the transformation history beneath an explanation.

The risk is not that any of these layers are unreal. A metric is real as a metric, a model is real as a model, and a narrative is real as a narrative. The risk appears when the system forgets what kind of object it is operating on and grants the representation the authority of the reality beneath it. The stack is useful because it makes those transformations visible and shows where corrective contact can weaken.

## Recursive Drift

Reality Drift becomes more difficult to recognize when representations begin changing the conditions they were originally created to describe. A system represents reality, acts on that representation, and then people and institutions adapt to the categories, incentives, measurements, or predictions it produces. Those adaptations alter the environment. When the altered environment is measured again, the new data may appear to confirm the representation that helped produce it.

This produces a recursive pattern:

**Reality → Representation → Proxy → Optimization → Behavioral Adaptation → Altered Reality → Recapture → Apparent Confirmation**

The system can eventually begin interpreting conditions produced by its own representations as independent evidence that those representations were correct. At its strongest, the relationship reverses. The representation is no longer primarily answerable to reality. Reality is increasingly reorganized around the representation.

## Why Drift Persists

One of the most important features of Reality Drift is that wrongness can be survivable. A representation does not have to be fully accurate in order for the institution, model, platform, or workflow using it to continue. Costs can be delayed, distributed, displaced onto other people, excluded from existing measurements, or absorbed as normal administrative friction. This allows systems to remain wrong for long periods without generating the kind of failure that would normally force correction.

This is why Reality Drift is different from ordinary error. An error can remain visible and correctable. Drift becomes significant when mismatch persists, compounds, and becomes compatible with continued operation. The system may actually become more coherent as contact weakens because ambiguity, contradiction, and uncertainty are being resolved at the level of representation without necessarily being resolved in the world.

## Provenance and Recursive Representation

Provenance becomes especially important in systems where representations are repeatedly reused. Reports summarize records, articles summarize reports, models train on articles, search systems retrieve those outputs, AI systems summarize them again, and institutions may incorporate the resulting material into later decisions. Each step can preserve useful information while also removing context and transformation history.

A citation alone does not necessarily preserve provenance. What matters is whether the path from source conditions to the current representation can still be reconstructed. When that path is lost, derivative claims can appear original, inherited assumptions can appear independently confirmed, and accumulated compression becomes difficult to distinguish from direct observation. In recursively generated environments, this can make correction especially difficult because later systems operate on earlier transformations without knowing what was removed or changed along the way.

## Related Concepts

Reality Drift is the systems-level condition that organizes the broader framework. The related concepts describe particular mechanisms, effects, or manifestations within it. Semantic Fidelity concerns whether meaning, intent, context, distinctions, and referential relationships survive transformation. Recursive Compression describes what happens when already compressed representations are reused as inputs to later cycles of interpretation or action. Constraint Collapse describes the loss of binding force in feedback and correction. The Optimization Trap describes a condition in which improvement within a representation becomes easier to demonstrate than improvement in the underlying reality.

Synthetic Realness describes the situation in which a polished or optimized representation produces stronger signals of credibility or completeness than the less legible reality it represents. Cognitive Drift applies the same general problem at the level of human models, attention, judgment, and interpretation. Filter Fatigue describes the burden placed on individuals who must continuously determine what is real, relevant, original, or sufficiently grounded. Temporal Drift concerns misalignment between representational time and the timing, sequence, or memory of underlying events. The Drifted Self describes identity under conditions of continuous representation and adaptation, while Co-Cognition examines cognition distributed across recursive exchanges among people, tools, institutions, and computational systems.

## A Practical Diagnostic

A useful way to investigate Reality Drift is to begin with the reality a system is supposed to remain answerable to and then trace the representations between that reality and the final decision or output. What does the system actually observe? What becomes measurable? Which proxy becomes operationally authoritative? What is being optimized? What disappears during transformation? Which feedback channels still have the power to force revision? Can the source conditions behind the output still be reconstructed?

The most important test is whether the system can report success while the underlying condition deteriorates. If represented performance can continue improving while the thing being represented gets worse, the system is structurally capable of Reality Drift.

Common warning signs include metrics improving while lived outcomes worsen, procedural completion substituting for substantive resolution, internal consistency being treated as evidence of external accuracy, derivative representations replacing original sources, contradictory evidence being absorbed as an exception rather than prompting revision, provenance becoming difficult to reconstruct, people adapting themselves to satisfy measurement systems, and feedback remaining abundant while rarely changing the categories, incentives, or decisions that organize the system.

No single sign proves Reality Drift. The pattern becomes significant when operational persistence, fidelity loss, and weakened correction appear together.

## What Reality Drift Is Not

Reality Drift is not synonymous with error, bias, corruption, disagreement, misinformation, or immediate system failure. It is also distinct from established concepts such as data drift, model drift, concept drift, policy drift, and genetic drift. Those phenomena may contribute to Reality Drift or resemble parts of its architecture, but they have their own domain-specific meanings.

The framework is concerned with a more general question: under what conditions can a representational system remain coherent and operational after the relationship between its internal logic and external reality has begun to weaken?

## In One Sentence

Reality Drift is the progressive loss of corrective contact between a system's representations and the real-world conditions they are meant to track, describe, govern, or serve, even while the system remains functional and internally coherent.

The broader framework examines what happens when representations become easier to produce, optimize, distribute, and reuse than the realities capable of correcting them. Representation is necessary. The problem begins when it stops functioning as a revisable interface with reality and becomes the environment within which the system evaluates itself.

Representation enables scale. Feedback preserves contact.
